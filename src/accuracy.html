How accurate the fog forecast is

The forecast was checked on 9,794 mornings since January 2024 in 22 cities in Europe, the United States and New Zealand. Fog came about as often as forecast.

On two mornings in three the chance given was about 1%, and fog came on 1% of them. Up to about 40% the chances held: mornings given about 39% had fog 41% of the time. The highest chances ran somewhat high: mornings given about 76% had fog 65% of the time.

How it was tested

For each morning, the forecast made the day before was compared with what was measured. No winter was tested with a formula that had seen it: each winter was left out when its formula was fitted.

Visibility is measured at weather stations, mostly airports, so the test was done there, with the weather data for the station. The station is written on each city's page. On the city pages the same formula is given the weather data for the city centre.

A morning was counted as foggy when visibility under 1 km, without rain, snow or drizzle, was measured in at least two of the six hours from 4 to 10 h. The mornings are from October to March, and from April to September in New Zealand.

Which fog it covers

The forecast is built for fog that forms overnight in calm air. Fog that comes in from the sea follows other rules, so coastal cities such as Los Angeles and San Francisco have no page.

Delhi, Lahore and Amritsar

These three share a formula of their own and were tested separately. It has one more input, whether the morning before had fog. Fog on that plain comes in runs of days: after a fog morning, the next one had fog about three times in four.

In Lahore the chances are close: mornings given about 60% had fog 58% of the time. In Delhi they run high in the middle of the range and are right at the top: mornings given about 60% had fog 43% of the time, and those given about 85% had it 88% of the time. In Amritsar they run low: mornings given about 60% had fog 80% of the time.

Compared with airport forecasts

For each airport a forecast is written by a forecaster (a TAF). At the six US airports on the site, the fog forecast of this site was compared with it on 2,584 mornings, 243 of them with fog, at the same number of fog warnings. A day ahead, more fog was caught by the formula than by the forecaster: 90 fog mornings against 81, with the airport forecast issued about 21 hours before. Against the one issued about 15 hours before, with six more hours of information, the two were level: 112 against 116. European airport forecasts are saved daily since October 2026, for the same comparison there.

How the percentage is made

The method is the one weather services use for local forecasts, called model output statistics: a logistic regression, fitted on two and a half winters of ICON forecasts against the visibility that airports reported. The method and all test results are on GitHub.

How accurate the fog forecast is

A fog chance of 20% is accurate if fog happens on about 20 of 100 mornings with that chance. This was checked for the forecast on 9,794 mornings since January 2024, in 22 cities in Europe, the United States and New Zealand.

Up to a chance of about 40%, fog happened as often as the forecast said, within two percentage points. Fog is rare, so most mornings are in the first row: a chance near zero, and almost no fog measured.

Above 50% the forecast was too high: mornings with a chance of about 76% had fog 65% of the time.

How it was tested

For each morning, the forecast made the day before was compared with what was measured. The forecast is a formula fitted on past winters. To keep the test fair, each winter was tested with a formula fitted on the other winters only, so every morning of the test was new to its formula.

A forecast like this needs two things. One is the weather model's data, such as humidity and wind, which exist for any place. The other is visibility measurements, which exist only at weather stations, mostly airports. So the formula was fitted and tested at the stations, where a forecast can be compared with what was measured. The station is written on each city's page.

On a city page the same formula is used with the weather model's data for the city centre. The link between weather and fog found at the station is taken to hold a few kilometres away as well.

A morning was counted as foggy when, in at least two of the six hours from 4 to 10 h, visibility was under 1 km with no rain, snow or drizzle. The mornings are from October to March, and from April to September in New Zealand.

Which fog it covers

The forecast is made for fog that forms overnight in calm air. Fog that comes in from the sea is a different kind, so coastal cities such as Los Angeles and San Francisco have no page.

Delhi, Lahore and Amritsar

These three cities have a formula of their own and were tested separately. Fog on that plain comes in runs of days: after a foggy morning, the next morning had fog about three times in four. So their formula has one more input, whether the morning before had fog.

Mornings with a chance of about 60% had fog 58% of the time in Lahore, 43% in Delhi and 80% in Amritsar. So the chance is accurate in Lahore, too high in Delhi and too low in Amritsar. At the top of the range Delhi is accurate: mornings with a chance of about 85% had fog 88% of the time.

Compared with airport forecasts

Airports have professional fog forecasts. Several times a day a forecaster writes down the visibility expected at the airport over the next day (a TAF). The forecast of this site was compared with them at the six US airports on the site, on 2,584 mornings, 243 of them with fog. Both were given the same number of fog warnings.

A day ahead the forecast of this site was a little ahead: it caught 90 of the fog mornings, and the airport forecast issued about 21 hours before caught 81. Airport forecasts are written for flight planning and renewed every six hours, and they are cautious about fog at their far end. The next airport forecast, issued about 15 hours before with six more hours of information, was level: it caught 116, against 112.

For the same comparison in Europe, airport forecasts are saved daily since October 2026.

How the percentage is made

The method is one that weather services use for local forecasts, called model output statistics. Two and a half winters of forecasts from the ICON weather model were put next to the visibility measured at airports, and a formula (a logistic regression) was fitted to them. The method and all test results are on GitHub.

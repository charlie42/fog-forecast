How accurate the fog forecast is

The forecast was checked on 9,794 mornings since January 2024 in 22 cities in Europe, the United States and New Zealand, at the weather station named on each city's page. The mornings are from October to March, and from April to September in New Zealand. For each morning, the forecast made the day before was compared with what happened. A morning was counted as foggy when visibility under 1 km, without rain, snow or drizzle, was measured at the station in at least two of the six hours from 4 to 10 h. Each winter was tested with a formula that was fitted without that winter.

The forecast works for fog that forms overnight in calm air. It does not work for fog that comes in from the sea, so coastal cities such as Los Angeles and San Francisco have no page.

Each forecast is for one weather station, usually the city's airport, and fog is local. The airport can be in fog while the city centre is clear, or the other way round. The station is written on each city's page.

Delhi, Lahore and Amritsar share a formula of their own and were tested separately. In Delhi the forecast gives too high a chance in the middle of the range: mornings given about 60% had fog 43% of the time, and those given about 85% had it 88% of the time. In Lahore it is close: mornings given about 60% had fog 58% of the time. In Amritsar it gives too low a chance: mornings given about 60% had fog 80% of the time.

How the percentage is made

The method is what weather services call model output statistics: a logistic regression, fitted on two and a half winters of ICON forecasts against the visibility that airports reported. Delhi, Lahore and Amritsar have one more input, whether the morning before had fog. Fog on that plain comes in runs of days: after a fog morning, the next one had fog about three times in four.

The method and all test results are on GitHub.

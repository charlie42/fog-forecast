Mist vs fog

The difference between mist and fog

Both are small water drops in the air near the ground. The name depends on how far one can see: under 1 km it is fog, from 1 to 5 km it is mist. In fog the other side of the street is gone. In mist the trees across a field are still there, paler with each row.

Much of what is called mist is fog by this measure. In the second picture the last trees one can make out are a few hundred metres away, so the visibility is under 1 km. In the third the hills are a few kilometres away and still there.

How often there is mist without fog

More often than fog. Airports measure visibility, so they can give us the numbers. At 14 European airports, mist without fog was measured on one morning in ten from October to March, and fog on one in thirteen. At Milan's airport it was almost one morning in four, in Berlin one in eight.

What a fog forecast shows on a misty morning

Weather apps show fog as yes or no, or as a percentage. There is nothing like that for mist, so before a misty morning the fog forecast is all there is to look at.

It is easy to think that a fog forecast is about fog only. It is not that strict. Since January 2024 there were 623 mornings with mist but no fog, and the fog forecast of the ICON weather model, made a day ahead, said "fog" on 45% of them. On mornings with real fog it said so on 74%, and on mornings with neither on 10%.

So a fog forecast does pick up mist, but only half of it. Whether a given misty morning is in the forecast is close to a coin toss.

A forecast with a number for mist

This site, Chance of Fog, gives a number for mist as well as for fog. On its forecast pages each morning has two numbers, a chance for mist and a chance for fog, and so has each hour from 3 to 11 h. On a morning given "mist 40%, fog 5%", fog is unlikely and there is mist two times in five.

In Delhi, Lahore and Amritsar smog alone often keeps the visibility under 5 km, so the measurements cannot tell mist from smog. These three cities have a chance for fog only.

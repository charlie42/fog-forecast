How to predict fog for photography

Morning fog forms when the air near the ground cools overnight until it can hold no more water, and no wind moves it. You can read most of that from the forecast the evening before.

Humidity

Look at the forecast humidity between 4 and 10 h. At airports in 14 European cities, mornings when the forecast said 95% or more had mist (visibility under 5 km) 37% of the time. The other mornings had it 6% of the time.

Wind, rain, cloud and dew point

The 37% changes with the rest of the forecast:

  • Wind under 4 km/h: 50%. Over 10 km/h: 15%.
  • No rain overnight: 43%. More than 1 mm: 18%.
  • Clear sky the evening before: 49%. Overcast: 33%.
  • Evening temperature within 1 °C of the dew point: 48%. More than 4 °C above it: 25%.

Time of morning

On misty mornings at Berlin's airport, 7 h was the mistiest hour, with mist on 83% of them. By 10 h it was down to 34%. Over five autumns at that airport, when mist was seen around 6 h, it was still there an hour later 78% of the time, and still there three hours later about half the time.

Place

Fog is local. Heathrow has mist on 7% of mornings. Stansted and Luton have it on 27%. On 3 October 2026 the Tempelhof station in Berlin reported 300 m visibility at 7 h while Dahlem, a few kilometres away, reported no fog at all.

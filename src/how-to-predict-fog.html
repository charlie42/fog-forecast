How to predict fog for photography

Morning fog forms when the air near the ground cools overnight until it can hold no more water, and no wind moves it. You can read most of that from the forecast the evening before.

Humidity

Look at the forecast humidity between 4 and 10 h. At airports in 14 European cities, mornings with a forecast humidity of 95% or more had mist (visibility under 5 km) 37% of the time. The other mornings had mist 6% of the time.

Wind, rain, cloud and dew point

The numbers below are only for the mornings with a forecast humidity of 95% or more. Of all those mornings, 37% had mist. The share was higher or lower depending on the rest of the forecast:

Time of morning

At Berlin's airport, of the mornings that had mist at any hour, 83% had it at 7 h, more than at any other hour. At 10 h, 34% still had it. Over five autumns at that airport, when mist was seen around 6 h, it was still there an hour later 78% of the time, and still there three hours later about half the time.

Place

Fog is local. Heathrow has mist on 7% of mornings. Stansted and Luton have it on 27%. On 3 October 2026 the Tempelhof station in Berlin reported 300 m visibility at 7 h while Dahlem, a few kilometres away, reported no fog at all.

How often fog happens

How often thick fog happens (visibility under 1 km for two hours or more) depends on the city. Of the European cities on the forecast pages, it is rarest in $lowest, on $low of mornings from October to March, and most common in $highest, on $high. Mist is usually two to three times as common.

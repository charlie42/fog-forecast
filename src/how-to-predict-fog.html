How to predict fog for photography

Morning fog forms when the air near the ground cools overnight until it can hold no more water, and nothing stirs it. You can read most of that from the forecast the evening before. The numbers below come from October to March mornings since 2024 at airports in 14 European cities, comparing the day-ahead forecast with the visibility that was reported. Mist here means visibility under 5 km.

Start with humidity

Look at the forecast humidity between 4 and 10 h. At Berlin's airport, mornings forecast to reach 95% or more had mist half the time. The other mornings had it 6% of the time.

Then wind, rain, cloud and dew point

Across the 14 cities, 37% of mornings that passed the humidity test had mist. The rest of the forecast moves that number:

  • Wind under 4 km/h: 50%. Over 10 km/h: 15%.
  • No rain overnight: 43%. More than 1 mm: 18%.
  • Clear sky the evening before: 49%. Overcast: 33%.
  • Evening temperature within 1 °C of the dew point: 48%. More than 4 °C above it: 25%.

Be early

On misty mornings at Berlin's airport, 7 h was the mistiest hour, with mist on 83% of them. By 10 h it was down to 34%. Over five autumns there, mist seen around 6 h was still there an hour later 78% of the time and three hours later about half the time.

Pick the place

Fog is local. Heathrow has mist on 7% of mornings, Stansted and Luton on 27%. On 3 October 2026 the Tempelhof station in Berlin reported 300 m visibility at 7 h while Dahlem, a few kilometres away, reported no fog at all.

Expect it to be rare
